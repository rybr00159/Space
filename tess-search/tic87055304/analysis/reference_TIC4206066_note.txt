           Two Transit-like Signals in TESS Photometry of the Nearby K Dwarf TIC 4206066
                                                     Pavel Rabtsevich1
                                                     1 Independent Researcher



                                                          ABSTRACT
          I report two transit-like signals in TESS photometry of the late-K dwarf TIC 4206066 (StKM 1-561),
        found in Sector 98 and traced back to Sectors 6 and 32. The 3.18 d signal (23 transits, ≈500 ppm)
        corresponds to a 1.4 ± 0.1 R⊕ planet if it orbits the target. Vetting, localization, and Gaia astrometry,
        photometry, and radial velocities leave a planet transiting an unresolved bound companion as the main
        alternative host scenario; TRICERATOPS gives FPP = 0.03–0.04, mostly from companions these data
        disfavor. The lower-significance 11.13 d signal (≈2.2 R⊕ if on the target; ≈ 3σ in a post hoc test) is
        reported with a tentative ephemeris.


                                                 1. TARGET AND DATA
  TIC 4206066 (Gaia DR3 3220388198192519424; T = 9.68) lies at 35.6 pc (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016; Gaia
Collaboration et al. 2023). From MKs = 4.94, the relations of A. W. Mann et al. (2015, 2019) give R⋆ = 0.623 ±
0.019 R⊙ , M⋆ = 0.606 ± 0.015 M⊙ , and ρ⋆ = 2.51+0.25
                                                   −0.22 ρ⊙ . I searched Sector 98 Quick-Look Pipeline (QLP) light
curves (C. X. Huang et al. 2020) of 126,246 stars (T ≤ 11.5) with box least squares (G. Kovács et al. 2002). Both
passed all LEO-Vetter flux-level tests (M. Kunimoto et al. 2025) but their multiple-event statistics (10.6, 11.0) fell
below the shortlist threshold (15), so I examined them post hoc. I use TESS (G. R. Ricker et al. 2015) light curves
from TESS-SPOC (Sector 6, 30 min PDCSAP; D. A. Caldwell et al. 2020), SPOC (Sector 32, 2 min PDCSAP; J. M.
Jenkins et al. 2016), and QLP (Sector 98, 200 s, TGLC-based; T. Han & T. D. Brandt 2023; G. Petitpas et al. 2026),
removing all flagged Sector 98 cadences.
                                                2. THE 3.18 DAY SIGNAL
  An epoch search at the Sector 98-only period recovers the signal in Sectors 6 and 32 (false-alarm probability ≲ 1%
per sector, ≲ 0.1% jointly, trials included). Transit times from a fixed-shape batman template (L. Kreidberg 2015)
with injection-calibrated errors give P = 3.182785 ± 0.000006 d and T0 = 2460998.736 ± 0.003 BJDTDB from the 19
transits with ≥70% coverage, S/N ≥ 2, and ≥15 min of post-egress data (χ2 /dof ≈ 9.5/17; no evidence of timing
variations). An exposure-integrated emcee fit (D. Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013) of all 23 transits (circular orbit; A.
                                                               +0.10
Claret 2017 limb darkening; Mann density prior) gives b = 0.35−0.14  , T14 = 1.87±0.05 h, and Rp /R⋆ = 0.0204±0.0010,
so Rp = 1.4 ± 0.1 R⊕ . With free density, ρ⋆ ≲ 3.6 ρ⊙ (97.7%). Per-sector and odd/even depths agree, the phase-0.5
depth is −20 ± 32 ppm, and a V shape is disfavored (∆χ2 = 32; ≥ 21 with red noise). Without Sector 6 these values
change by ≲ 1σ.
                                               3. THE 11.13 DAY SIGNAL
  A lower-significance 11.13 d signal (≈1000 ppm; Rp ≈ 2.2 R⊕ if on the target; offset (+1 ± 3, −1 ± 3)′′ ) has two clean
Sector 98 transits, which alone do not establish it, and dips consistent with its period in Sector 6 (marginally in Sector
32), at ≈ 3σ (false-alarm probability ≈ 2 × 10−3 ) in a recovery test defined after inspecting the archival data, where
an unanchored search had failed. Its Sector 32 events abut 3.18 d transits; assuming the archival dips are real, its
ephemeris is P = 11.13274 ± 0.00003 d and T0 = 2461000.865 ± 0.003 BJDTDB .
                                      4. LOCALIZATION AND FALSE POSITIVES
  SPOC (Sector 32, pointing-decorrelated) and TESScut (Sector 98; C. E. Brasseur et al. 2019) difference images fitted
with the TESS pixel response function (K. J. Bell & M. E. Higgins 2022) place the 3.18 d source at (+5 ± 3, 0 ± 3)′′

from the target (≈ 2σ; ≤ 2.6σ across Sector 98 reductions). Sector 6 difference images are unusable (transits lie
0.4–3.5 h from momentum dumps). All Gaia DR3 stars able to produce the depth lie ≳ 43′′ (> 10σ) away. Gaia
DR3 gives RUWE = 1.21 and radial-velocity scatter (p = 4 × 10−5 ) that are elevated (origin unknown) but below
the RUWE (1.4) and gof (3.0 vs. 4) thresholds, no non-single-star solution or other source within 10′′ , and radial
velocities (K < 1.2 km s−1 ) that exclude an eclipsing stellar companion of the target. Against the 0.05–0.07 mag
scatter of comparable stars, the XP overluminosity of Z. Way et al. (2026), +0.003 mag, disfavors Gaia-blended
(≲ 0.2′′ ) companions above ≈ 0.38 M⊙ (2σ; M. J. Pecaut & E. E. Mamajek 2013); at 0.2–2′′ such companions would
be expected to raise the BP/RP flux excess (M. Riello et al. 2021), which is normal. For the 3.18 d duration, a circular
orbit needs a companion host above ≈ 0.52 M⊙ ; under the adopted companion model, 0.15–0.38 M⊙ hosts would
require e ≳ 0.2–0.6.
  TRICERATOPS 1.1.0 (S. Giacalone et al. 2021), with Sector 32+98 folds (5–10 min bins) and unresolved companions
at 0.1–2.2′′ , gives FPP = 0.03–0.04 (4–20 runs of 106 draws), nearly all from bound companions, about 90% from those
above 0.38 M⊙ ; excluding these (post hoc) gives FPP ≈ 0.002–0.007. The default FPP exceeds the TRICERATOPS
validation threshold (FPP < 0.015, NFPP < 10−3 ; here NFPP < 10−6 ), so I claim no validation.

                                                                                          5. STATUS
  The 3.18 d signal is a planet candidate whose main remaining false-positive channel needs high-resolution imaging
and radial velocities; the 11.13 d signal is a tentative (≈ 3σ, post hoc) signal. Sector 110 (2026 October 31–November
26) spans eight or nine 3.18 d transit times (one in a scheduled data gap) and three 11.13 d ones. As of 2026 September
25 (UTC), I found no prior report of either signal in ExoFOP, TCE tables, the NASA Exoplanet Archive, candidate
catalogs, SIMBAD, VizieR, ADS, or arXiv.
